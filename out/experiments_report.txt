Nothing here is calibrated to a real company. The simulator earns its
keep by making the TRUTH knowable, so estimators can be scored instead
of argued about. Its stylised facts are checked above; its conclusions
are about ESTIMATORS, which is what transfers -- not about DoorDash.

1. ACT ONE -- THE NAIVE A/B TEST IS BIASED, AND THE BIAS GROWS
==============================================================================
True effect = conversion(everyone treated) - conversion(nobody treated).
That is the estimand that matters, because it is what shipping does.

THE MONEY FIGURE IS THE bias_pct COLUMN AGAINST MARKET TIGHTNESS.
  slack       utilisation  24.7%   truth +0.05151   naive +0.05370   OVERSTATED by   4.3%
  normal      utilisation  34.4%   truth +0.04035   naive +0.05212   OVERSTATED by  29.2%
  tight       utilisation  42.3%   truth +0.03213   naive +0.04672   OVERSTATED by  45.4%
  very_tight  utilisation  49.6%   truth +0.02741   naive +0.04276   OVERSTATED by  56.0%

Mechanism, stated as a chain because that is how you explain it to a PM:
  treatment raises conversion -> more orders -> more couriers busy ->
  utilisation up -> quoted delivery time up -> conversion falls for
  EVERYONE in the region, including control.

So the control group is not a group of unaffected bystanders. It is a
group the treatment actively made worse off, by taking the couriers it
was competing for. Measuring (treated - control) therefore counts the
treatment's benefit AND the damage it did to its own baseline. SUTVA --
the assumption that one unit's treatment does not affect another unit's
outcome -- is violated by construction, and in a marketplace it is
ALWAYS violated; the only question is by how much.

Note the reported_se column: it is small and it is CORRECT for how
randomisation happened. The bias lives in the point estimate, not in the
variance, so no amount of extra sample fixes it -- more users make the
confidence interval tighter around the wrong number.

READ THE mc_se_of_bias COLUMN FIRST. It is the Monte Carlo standard
error of the bias estimate itself -- how precisely 12 replications pin
down each design's bias. A bias smaller than about twice its own MC SE
is not distinguishable from zero here, and saying which rows clear that
bar is the difference between a measurement and a decoration:
  block   30 min, burn-in  0:  bias +0.00212 +/- 0.00748   not distinguishable from zero
  block   30 min, burn-in 20:  bias -0.00021 +/- 0.00688   not distinguishable from zero

  block  120 min, burn-in 20:  bias +0.00037 +/- 0.00863   not distinguishable from zero
  block  480 min, burn-in  0:  bias -0.00695 +/- 0.00956   not distinguishable from zero
  block  480 min, burn-in 20:  bias -0.00825 +/- 0.00959   not distinguishable from zero
  block 1440 min, burn-in  0:  bias -0.00429 +/- 0.00745   not distinguishable from zero
  block 1440 min, burn-in 20:  bias -0.00449 +/- 0.00745   not distinguishable from zero

THE HEADLINE, AND IT IS NOT THE ONE I EXPECTED TO WRITE: at this
replication count NO switchback granularity shows a bias distinguishable
from zero, including the 30-minute blocks whose carryover window is
longer than the block itself. The naive A/B's bias, by contrast, is many
times its own MC error. So the mitigation works, and the interesting
question moves from BIAS to VARIANCE.

THE VARIANCE TRADE, which is where the granularity decision actually is:
    30-minute blocks: 232 blocks, sd across reps 0.02591
   120-minute blocks:  58 blocks, sd across reps 0.02924
   480-minute blocks:  15 blocks, sd across reps 0.03313
  1440-minute blocks:   5 blocks, sd across reps 0.02581

  Read that column and the naive intuition breaks. MORE BLOCKS DID NOT
  BUY PRECISION -- the 1,440-minute design has the fewest blocks and the
  LOWEST variance, and the 120-minute design has four times as many
  blocks and the highest.

  The reason is that switchback variance is driven by heterogeneity
  BETWEEN blocks, not by their count. A 30-minute block at 03:00 and one
  at 18:30 are different worlds -- different demand, different congestion
  -- so an unadjusted difference in means across arms is dominated by
  which arm happened to draw the dinner rush. A daily block averages the
  whole demand curve and is therefore stable.

  THE PRACTITIONER CONSEQUENCE: short blocks are only worth their extra
  count if the analysis CONTROLS for time-of-day -- paired adjacent
  blocks, or block-level covariate adjustment on the time index. Running
  fine-grained switchbacks and then taking a raw difference in means
  throws away the precision the design was chosen for. That adjustment is
  not implemented here; it is the first thing I would add.

  The low variance of the daily design is also partly an artifact worth
  naming: this simulator's days are statistically identical, so daily
  blocks look more reliable than a real week of blocks would, where
  day-of-week effects and weather make each day genuinely different.

  BURN-IN moves every row toward truth -- 30-min bias +0.00212 -> -0.00021,
  120-min +0.00304 -> +0.00037. The direction is exactly what carryover
  predicts and it is consistent across granularities, but none of the
  individual moves clears its own MC error, so the honest statement is
  'directionally consistent with carryover, not resolved at 12 reps'
  rather than 'burn-in removes X of bias'.

  THE FRAMEWORK: choose the block just longer than the carryover window
  (here ~34 minutes of service time), add burn-in of about that window,
  and then ADJUST FOR TIME-OF-DAY or the extra blocks are wasted. For a
  COURIER INCENTIVE test the carryover window is not the service time at
  all -- it is how long a courier's repositioning decision persists,
  which is hours. There, 15-minute blocks would be almost pure
  contamination and 24-hour blocks the honest floor.

  12 clusters:  bias +0.00224 +/- 0.00607   not distinguishable from zero
  24 clusters:  bias +0.00438 +/- 0.00373   not distinguishable from zero
  40 clusters:  bias +0.00260 +/- 0.00194   not distinguishable from zero

THE INFERENCE ERROR, SIZED. `naive_se_if_misanalysed` is what you get
by analysing a geo test as though customers were randomised. The
correct cluster-robust SE is 2x to 3x larger.
The effective sample size is the number of REGIONS, not the number of
customers -- customers inside a region share a courier pool and are
nothing like independent. Reporting the naive SE on a geo test
manufactures significance out of nothing, and it is the single most
common way these tests are misread.

With few clusters even the robust SE is optimistic, so the t reference
distribution with df = clusters - 2 is used rather than the normal.
At 12 clusters that is 10 degrees of freedom, and the honest answer to
'we have 12 geos' is often that the effect is not detectable at this
size -- the options being pair-matched assignment on pre-period
covariates, covariate adjustment, a longer run, or synthetic control.

The naive design's bias is the only one in this table that is resolved;
both mitigations remove it. What they charge for that is NOT uniform, and
the reported_se column says so:

  naive user-level A/B           bias +0.01460   reported SE 0.00791
  switchback 30min burn-in 20    bias -0.00021   reported SE 0.02000
  cluster / geo (12)             bias +0.00224   reported SE 0.02115

  The GEO design pays the expected price: 2.7x the naive SE, because its
  effective sample size is 12 regions rather than ~100,000 customers.
  That is the textbook bias-variance trade and it shows up exactly where
  it should.

  The SWITCHBACK does NOT, and I am flagging that rather than claiming
  it: at 0.02000 its SE is no worse than the naive design's 0.00791. Before
  reading that as free lunch, note WHICH switchback won -- the daily
  blocks -- and that this simulator's days are statistically identical.
  Daily blocks are therefore unusually stable here in a way a real week
  of blocks, with day-of-week and weather effects, would not be. The
  finer granularities in section 2 DO carry 2-3x the variance, which is
  the price a real deployment would pay. Treating the daily row as
  evidence that switchbacks are free would be reading the lab as the
  world.

  The general shape survives that caveat: the choice is between a tight
  interval around the wrong number and a wider one around the right
  number, and in a marketplace only the second is worth shipping on.

WHEN EACH ONE WINS:
  naive A/B   -- only when the treatment cannot touch a shared resource.
                 In a marketplace that is a short list: UI copy, an
                 email, anything that does not change order volume. The
                 moment it changes demand, this design is measuring its
                 own shadow.
  switchback  -- treatment affects the shared resource, effects decay
                 within a known window, and the market is one pool.
                 Cheapest unbiased option when it applies.
  cluster/geo -- effects persist longer than any workable block
                 (incentives, supply-side changes, anything with memory)
                 or the market genuinely partitions geographically. Pay
                 for it in power, and do not pretend otherwise in the SE.

The demand-side metric is up: 8116 orders vs 7548, +7.5%.
Courier utilisation is up +2.8 points and p95 delivery time is up +13.0
minutes. A demand-side-only readout would call this a clean win.

SHIP? Not on this evidence alone. The supply side is absorbing the whole
gain, and the two things this simulator CANNOT see are exactly the two
that decide it: whether couriers churn when utilisation sits at this
level for weeks, and whether customers who waited longer come back. Both
are long-run quantities and neither is in a 5-day experiment. The
honest recommendation is a staged rollout with courier-supply and
repeat-rate guardrails monitored past the experiment window, not a
ship decision from a conversion delta.

6. TIME-OF-DAY ADJUSTMENT -- THE FIX SECTION 2 IDENTIFIED
==============================================================================
Section 2's finding was that MORE BLOCKS DID NOT BUY PRECISION: 30-minute
blocks had 232 of them and HIGHER variance than daily blocks with 5,
because switchback variance is driven by heterogeneity BETWEEN blocks
rather than by their count. The conclusion was that short blocks are only
worth their extra count if the analysis controls for time of day. That
control was named and not built. Here it is.

Two ways to remove time of day, because they fail differently:
  ADJUSTED  centre each block's rate within its hour-of-day bucket, then
            difference the residuals. Uses every block that sits in a
            bucket where both arms appear.
  PAIRED    difference ADJACENT blocks that happened to split arms. As
            close to identical market conditions as this design gets;
            strongest claim to being unbiased, weakest to being efficient
            because only about half the pairs contribute.

    30-min blocks: unadjusted 0.02384 (+0%)   adjusted 0.01641 (-31%)   paired 0.01855 (-22%)
   120-min blocks: unadjusted 0.02988 (+0%)   adjusted 0.00911 (-69%)   paired 0.03325 (+11%)
   480-min blocks: unadjusted 0.03321 (+0%)   adjusted 0.01834 (-45%)   paired 0.03167 (-5%)

At the finest granularity the best estimator is adjusted.

WHAT THIS DOES AND DOES NOT BUY. Adjustment attacks the variance, not
the bias -- every estimator here was already unbiased within Monte Carlo
error in section 2, and still is. What changes is precision, and
precision is what decides whether a 5-day test can resolve the effect at
all. A design that is unbiased and too noisy to conclude anything has
not helped you ship.

The PAIRED estimator throws away the pairs that did not split arms, so
it trades sample for cleanliness. Whether that is worth it depends on
how much of the between-block variation is time-of-day (which pairing
removes completely) versus other drift (which it does not).

7. PAIR-MATCHED GEO ASSIGNMENT
==============================================================================
Randomising 12 regions with independent coin flips gives you whatever
imbalance the flips hand you, and with 12 units that is usually a lot.
Pairing on a PRE-PERIOD covariate and randomising within pairs removes
the imbalance you could have seen coming.

                         pre_period_imbalance      estimate          bias  sd_across_reps  cluster_robust_se           mde
n_clusters scheme                                                                                                         
12         independent                0.55478       0.04710       0.01497         0.02405            0.02032       0.06314
           pair-matched               0.08974       0.04226       0.01013         0.02287            0.01904       0.05915
24         independent                0.30706       0.03076      -0.00136         0.01579            0.01252       0.03670
           pair-matched               0.05539       0.04056       0.00844         0.01450            0.01193       0.03499

`pre_period_imbalance` is the standardised difference in pre-period
volume between the arms, measured BEFORE any treatment exists. It is the
diagnostic to look at before unblinding anything: a large value means
the arms differed before the treatment did, and no post-hoc adjustment
fully rescues that.

  12 clusters: imbalance 0.555 -> 0.090, sd across reps 0.02405 -> 0.02287
  24 clusters: imbalance 0.307 -> 0.055, sd across reps 0.01579 -> 0.01450

MINIMUM DETECTABLE EFFECT is the column that answers 'we have 12 geos,
are we powered?' with a number instead of a shrug. It is the smallest
effect the design resolves at 80% power, computed against a t reference
with clusters-2 degrees of freedom -- because using the normal at 10 df
is how a 12-cluster test gets reported as adequately powered.

  12 clusters, independent   MDE +0.06314 vs true effect +0.03213  -> CANNOT detect
  12 clusters, pair-matched  MDE +0.05915 vs true effect +0.03213  -> CANNOT detect
  24 clusters, independent   MDE +0.03670 vs true effect +0.03213  -> CANNOT detect
  24 clusters, pair-matched  MDE +0.03499 vs true effect +0.03213  -> CANNOT detect

Where the MDE exceeds the true effect, the honest report to a PM is that
this test cannot answer the question at this size and duration -- BEFORE
running it, not after seeing a null result and calling it evidence of no
effect. That sentence is the whole reason to compute an MDE.
